## Reference evidence and its limits

[Tsukamoto et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19292484/), retained PubMed abstract, reports natural E isolation and its role as a short-lived metabolite in the marine-derived fungus. The primary full text could not be inspected in this attempt. Its abstract supports occurrence and identity; it does not assign a locus.

[Ding et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2941195/), Figure 1a/b, Results subsection on cluster localization, and Scheme 2, connects the producing MF297-2 reference fungus and its isolated notoamide products, including E, to the assigned cluster. The paper experimentally supports early cluster activities but leaves the E-forming part of the account proposed. Its assigned pathway is broader than the reactions individually tested.

[Li et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22188465/), retained PubMed abstract and publisher abstract, provides an exact-E reference-enzyme experiment: E is consumed and C/D are the identified products. That confirms product-specific participation in the assigned reference pathway; it is not a direct assay of E production. The retained [Fraley et al. 2020 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7483341/), opening paragraph and substrate-scope discussion accompanying Scheme 2, agrees with that interpretation. No precursor-incorporation outcome producing E was verified here.

The [accepted B decision](../../protocol/decisions/2026-10-03-notoamide-delegated-admission.md) permits a combined published assignment with partial experimental support, without requiring complete enzyme characterization. Applying that rule consistently, endogenous exact E in the assigned reference producer plus its experimentally demonstrated participation supports E as a reference-associated pathway product. This is a curator inference from the combined sources, not a direct native-locus perturbation result. Requiring a separately characterized E-forming enzyme here would introduce a stronger mechanistic gate than that decision uses. Conversely, the E-consuming assay alone would not justify admission.
