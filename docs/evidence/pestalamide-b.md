# Pestalamide B, source NRPSPKS_85

Curated 30 September 2026. Researcher-led scientific disposition is pending Vin Vomero's review; no external reviewer is assigned. Proposed evidence class: **directly supported GCF relationship, pending scientific review**. A member of the source family was experimentally tested. This does not establish production by every member or make the whole family ready for scoring.

[Caesar et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10313767/) purified pestalamide B from Aspergillus brasiliensis CBS 101.740 and identified it using MS and NMR (main manuscript pages 7–8; Supplementary Table 7 and Figures 20a, 21–25). The paper cloned the CBS 101.740 pst cluster into A. nidulans; expression produced pestalamide B, and deletion of pstD in that heterologous system removed production (Figure 4a). These experiments provide pathway-product evidence beyond the correlation that selected the family. They do not amount to a native CBS 101.740 knockout, which is unnecessary for the stated direct-GCF rule.

The main text calls the family HYBRID_85; Supplementary Table 6 and the deposited HTML call it NRPSPKS_85. Table 6 reports the same producer and nonproducer comparison. This is the documented alias used here, not a new clustering run. Table 8 identifies PstD as OJJ66275.1, 3,768 aa. The actual NRPSPKS_85.html includes that accession, with that full length, in KV878700.1.region001 / FMGX0018; its surrounding OJJ66272.1–OJJ66286.1 proteins include the pst cassette. The extracted PstD sequence SHA-256 is `6efcf4caed9b7390019ececfef09442f1611e5e6a307a91e976b0f2ddcd9ee6e`.

Caesar Supplementary Table 2 maps CBS 101.740 to GCA_001889945.1, and the current [NCBI assembly record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/genome/GCA_001889945.1/) identifies Aspergillus brasiliensis CBS 101740. The retained author-workbook crosswalk maps FMGX0018 to the same strain and assembly. The public table and protein accessions support the biological connection; the author crosswalk remains a separately identified historical source, not public experimental confirmation.

The principal alternatives were tested in the study. NRPKS_59/epa had been proposed elsewhere but is absent from the top producer. TERPENE_288 had an even stronger correlation, yet correlation alone did not establish the chemistry. The cloned pst result supports NRPSPKS_85 despite those alternatives. It does not justify assigning all congeners to the same precise structure or treating family-bearing, feature-low strains as negatives.
