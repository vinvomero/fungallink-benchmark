# Benzomalvin additional-target review

The retained 2017 FAC paper, DOI 10.1038/nchembio.2408, reports additional signals putatively assigned as benzomalvins E and B. Its Figure 2 caption describes deletion-dependent loss; Supplementary Figure 6 on printed page16 was visually inspected in this pass. The smaller bars, especially B, are not suitable for extracting numerical abundances from this overview. The paper identifies E/B by accurate mass and proposes host oxidation or spontaneous conversion. These are not authenticated identities, and the existing A/D standard must not be transferred to them. Neither target is admitted by this review.

## New primary follow-up

Clevenger et al. (2018), DOI [10.1021/acs.biochem.8b00076](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biochem.8b00076), [PMC5997561](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5997561/), was retrieved through the literature plugin's versioned PMC metadata and public XML URL. The metadata reports version1, an author manuscript, TDM licensing, no retraction and no open-access designation. No redistribution permission is inferred.

The source's Figure2B/C and accompanying text compare the free linear Anth-NmPhe-Anth tripeptide with synthetic compound3 by tandem mass spectrometry, including a diagnostic fragment. Figure2A describes measurements in the parent FAC and two altered constructs. This supplies a stronger chemical-evidence lead than the E/B mass assignments. It is one candidate measured intermediate, not an additional independent pathway or a new admission. This pass read XML text and captions; it did not visually inspect the2018 Figure2 panels or retrieve their underlying measurements.

The source explicitly leaves enzyme assignments for B, C and E to future work. It therefore does not close those assignments or supply a complete candidate-feature matrix. Desmethyl benzomalvin and a macrocyclic intermediate also appear, but their identities and distinct measurement keys were not adjudicated here. Do not count all mentioned compounds as eligible links.
